Black Hole Physics

Research on the formation and growth of galactic black holes

Image source: EHT Collaboration.

Black holes (BHs) are one of the most mysterious objects in the universe. Theory showed they form from gravitational collapse of massive stars, observations also showed that galaxies can host supermassive black holes (SMBHs) in their core. So, how exactly do they evolve and settled in the galaxy nucleus? We are interested in answering such question by using imaging and spectroscopy data from various facilities (such as HST, JWST, ALMA, ELT) to extract information from the environment around extragalactic BHs. We use analysis techniques such as stellar kinematics and gas dynamics to probe the BH's influence and model its properties. We are also exploring with using gravitational lensing as a way to extend the reach to even more distant and ancient galaxies.

Our works focuses on measuring SMBHs and hunting for intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs), which are the missing pieces in our understanding of the BH growth. Some key questions include: What is the origin for BH seeds in the early universe? How do SMBHs evolved from stellar mass? What do IMBHs tell us about the BH evolution chain? How can we use next-generation telescopes to probe the properties of intermediate-mass BHs (IMBHs)?

  • Key words: intermediate-mass black holes, supermassive black holes, stellar kinematics and dynamics.

Galaxy Evolution

Research on the evolution and properties of galactic bodies

Galaxies are large and complex systems, composed of stars, dust, and matter. We are living in a galaxy, the Milky Way, and we looked beyond it to find an impressive variety of systems with different shapes and sizes. We also discovered that BHs can reside in the compact cores of galaxies, including our own. These galactic BHs exerts gravitational influences that can consume nearby stars, alter their movements, and sometimes eject gas and matter into the circumgalactic medium through strong jets. Such kind of feedback can heavily affect the global properties of galaxies, like their morphology and star formation activity.

By probing deep into the galactic core and study the BH inside, we aim to establish a better understanding of how BHs co-evolve with their host galaxies and how the BH's impact on nearby environment can drive the processes happening deep within the galaxy. In addition, we are interested in seeing how galaxies of different mass scales evolved by analysing their stellar population and overall dynamics of their components.

  • Key words: dwarf galaxies, active galactic nuclei, nuclear star cluster, morphology.

Facilities & Instruments

Main telescopes used in our works

Telescopes are an indispensible part of astronomical research. Depending on the facility and instrument, the data product can have different properties, thus encoding different physical information. Our team uses many kinds of observational facilities, including...

Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)
ALMA is a large radio telescope array situated in Chile. The 66 antennas can collect signal from a broad range of frequencies, effectively capturing the emission of cold gas and dust. Using data from ALMA, we can extract the movement of molecular gas under the black hole's gravitational influence. This observation allow us to indirectly "see" the central black hole and then derive its properties.

James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)
JWST is a modern space telescope launched in 2021, currently situated at the Sun-Earth Lagrangian 2 point. On board the telescope is a variety of instruments designed to observe infrared range, such as NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS. These instruments enable high-resolution imaging and spectroscopy measurement of faint distant and ancient objects. Using data from JWST, we can probe kinematics of stars inside compact galactic cores, allowing better constraints of BH parameters.

ESO's Extremely Large Telescope (ELT)
The ELT is a next-generation ground based telescope currently underconstruction in Chile. With a 39-meter primary mirror and assistance from adaptive optics technology, ELT will be the largest telescope in the world, offering unprecedented observation capability. Our current work with this facility involves using simulation softwares to generate mock spectroscopy cubes from the HARMONI and MICADO instruments. Such studies help assess the performance of ELT and also evaluate future observational strategies for finding IMBHs population in our universe.

Keck Observatory
Keck is a complex of 2 telescopes located near the top of Mt. Mauna Kea, which is known for being one of the best astronomical observing sites in the world. This combines with Keck's arsenal of advanced adaptive optics and instruments like OSIRIS and KCWI offers incredible scientific data quality. Using Keck, we zoom deep into the bright and dense nuclear star clusters of nearby galaxies to better understand black hole-galaxy co-evolution. Additionally, we make use of Keck's high-resolution data of galaxies to derive their stellar population and dark matter properties, providing detailed insights on their formation and growth processes.

Giant Magellan Telescope (GMT)
The GMT is a next-generation ground based telescope currently underconstruction in Chile.

  • Key words: optical astronomy, infrared astronomy, radio astronomy, photometry, spectroscopy.
